Research

Knowledge engineering in support of the semantic interoperability of intelligent systems: devices, buildings, energy networks and data spaces.

Themes

My work answers one question: how can knowledge engineering support semantic interoperability between intelligent systems? The question has changed status. Under the EU Data Act, applicable since September 2025, semantic interoperability is a legal requirement, and the standardisation request of the European Commission names SAREF and DCAT-AP among the standards to use. What remains is to make it practical: for people who are not ontology experts, in systems with their own constraints, and with ontologies that must live for decades.

I treat the question as end-to-end engineering, from the model to its use, through the tools that produce it, the implementations that carry it and the standards that publish it. Results are established by measurement, by construction, and by adoption.

  • Describe: ontologies for intelligent systems. Reference ontologies (SAREF, SOSA/SSN, SEAS) and the patterns that circulate between them; project ontologies built ahead of them, such as CoSWoT and its Kinds of X and X of Interest pattern, and the Common Semantic Data Model of the OMEGA-X energy data space.
  • Produce: ontology engineering. What breaks when versioned ontologies evolve in networks, agile and tooled methodologies (ACIMOV, AIME, OLIVAW), and the SAREF development and publication framework, now part of a European standard.
  • Exchange: data and services on the Web. Lifting, lowering and validation between heterogeneous formats and RDF (SPARQL-Generate), custom datatypes (cdt:ucum), tools for developers, content negotiation, and the negotiation of data access contracts in data spaces.
  • Apply: the building as an intelligent system. The W3C Linked Building Data community (BOT, OPM), automated compliance checking (ACCORD), the digital twin of our building (Databat), and simulation for the Internet of Things, down to simulations on constrained devices.
